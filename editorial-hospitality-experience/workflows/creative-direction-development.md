# Develop a distinct editorial hospitality direction

1. Inventory existing identity, technical stack, property attributes, available media, audience, and page purposes. Record missing evidence rather than fabricating it.
2. Produce a design thesis (50–100 words), 3–5 visual principles, and 3–5 purposeful anti-principles.
3. Define composition and imagery strategy per context: homepage, room PDP, rate comparison, checkout, concierge, mobile.
4. Specify typography roles, color/contrast pairs, spacing and aspect-ratio tokens, motion rules, and content density rules; preserve existing standards unless the brief authorizes change.
5. Generate two meaningfully different art directions using the same factual/functional requirements, then compare their clarity and distinction.
6. Test problematic content: extended room names, long policy copy, low-quality photography, sold-out inventory, errors, reduced motion, narrow mobile.
7. Present rationale, risks, implementation mapping, and recommended experiments. Label aesthetic preferences as judgment, not measured conversion findings.
